# Keepsake publishing operations

## Source of truth

`keepsake-builder.html` **in this repo** is the only supported keepsake template and generator. The old hand-edited template is retired, and so is the copy of the builder that briefly lived in the `keepsakes` repo — it predated the consent/page-code/downloads upgrade and pointed its CTA at tinyurl. Client pages and examples must be generated by this builder so privacy metadata, media downloads, accessibility behavior, and brand styling stay consistent.

Delivered keepsake pages are published in the separate `keepsakes` repo (served at `/keepsakes/<page-code>/`), keeping client work out of this marketing repo.

## The audio-first layout (October 2026)

The client hears the song for the first time **on the keepsake page**, not on YouTube. A page built now opens with the album art, then straight under it a large **Play the song** button over the native player (`preload="metadata"`), then **Download your song**, which saves as `[Song Title] - Heartstrings Studio.mp3`. Story, lyrics, the lyric-sheet download and the share button follow as before.

- **Share video** (required, the MP4 as a Google Drive share link set to "Anyone with the link"): shows a **Watch the video** button under the download, and a **Download your share video** item in the keepsake downloads (Drive links are turned into a direct download). Every client is promised the MP4, so the builder won't finish a page without it. The MP4 itself lives on Drive, never in the page's folder.
- **YouTube link** (optional): a small **Also on YouTube** text link at the very bottom of the page. YouTube is never embedded and never sits above the player.

A keepsake is now a **folder of three files**, and the builder's download saves all three: `index.html`, `art.jpg` (cropped square, at most 1600px) and `song.mp3`. They go in `keepsakes/<page-code>/` with those exact names. Keep `song.mp3` under 15 MB (re-export at 192 kbps); the builder warns over that. In the `keepsakes` repo, `python3 tools/check_keepsakes.py` fails if a page's `song.mp3` or `art.jpg` is missing or a video file is present, and warns when the site passes 800 MB of the 1 GB GitHub Pages cap. Step-by-step instructions are in that repo's `tools/README.md`.

Keepsakes delivered before this keep the old single-file layout and are not migrated. Generated pages link the Story Room directly and carry the studio's GA4 tag.

## First listen, then keepsake

The builder's **Page type** switch (top of the form) builds one of two pages for the same song:

- **First listen**: the client's premiere. Album art, the song on the player and the lyrics (opened). No story, no **Download your song**, no keepsake downloads (MP3, lyric sheet, share video), no **Watch the video**, no share button, no YouTube link. The player asks browsers not to offer a download (`controlslist="nodownload"`). In the builder, the fields this page doesn't use (YouTube link, share video, lyric-sheet PDF, story) are greyed out and locked; anything already typed in them is kept for the keepsake build.
- **Keepsake delivery** (the default): the full page described above.

Both publish to the same `keepsakes/<page-code>/` folder, so the client keeps one link. To deliver, flip the switch, download again and replace `index.html` (and `song.mp3` if the song changed after revisions). Save a draft after the first listen so the delivery build starts from it.

The first-listen page **hides the downloads, it doesn't lock the song**: `song.mp3` still sits at a public address beside the page, the same caveat as `?share` below.

## The share button

Clients want to show the song to family and friends without handing out the download links with it. Every generated page carries a **Share this song** button above the footer, and it hands out the page's own address with `?share` on the end:

```
/keepsakes/<page-code>/          the client's link, downloads and all
/keepsakes/<page-code>/?share    the one they send on
```

Opened with that marker, the page hides the **Download your song** button and the **Keepsake downloads** section (lyric sheet and share-video download); the **Watch the video** button stays. The player stays, so whoever the client sends it to can hear the song (older single-file pages hid their player too). The story, lyrics, artwork and links are unchanged, and the page swaps a line of text so the client is told what the shared link leaves out while the recipient just gets the song.

`#share` works as well as `?share`.

**This hides; it does not remove.** On audio-first pages `song.mp3` sits at a public address beside the page, and the player can be saved with a right-click; on older pages the MP3 and PDF are inside the one file. A shared link opened with JavaScript off shows the downloads, and a determined reader could pull both files out of the page source. It is what the client hands out, not a privacy boundary — the same caveat as `noindex` below. 

Keepsakes delivered before this landed can be brought up to date with `tools/add_share_button.py` in the `keepsakes` repo.

## Required delivery checklist

Every delivered keepsake must contain:

- an opaque page code and filename;
- `noindex, nofollow` metadata;
- approved title, dedication, story, artwork, lyrics, and (optionally) alt text — left blank, it reads "Album art for <title>.";
- `art.jpg` (square album art) and `song.mp3` (under 15 MB) in the page's folder, next to `index.html`;
- the song playing on the page, with a **Download your song** button;
- the MP4 share video, as a Google Drive link (watch button and download);
- optional: an unlisted YouTube link;
- a printable lyric-sheet PDF;
- an explicit client publishing approval check.

## Privacy boundary

`noindex` prevents ordinary search indexing; it does not make a page private. Neither does the share button's `?share` marker: it hides the download links, it does not remove the files from the page. A public Git repository also preserves names and stories in git history.

Before publishing real client work:

1. Use first names unless the client explicitly approves more.
2. Review the story for addresses, contact details, medical details, and other sensitive information.
3. Keep real client keepsakes out of the public marketing repository.
4. Publish them through a separate service with access control when privacy is promised.
5. Do not use a client-side JavaScript password screen as security.

Until separate hosting is configured, the builder can create and preview a finished file, but publishing that file to this public repository requires deliberate client approval.

## Drafts

Draft files are downloaded locally and can include the complete story, artwork, MP3, and PDF. Store them as client-confidential files and delete them when no longer needed.
